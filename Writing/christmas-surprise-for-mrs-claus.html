Christmas Story

A Christmas Surprise for Mrs. Claus

By Karen Hodges

★

Santa has found gifts for everyone except Mrs. Claus—until he realizes that the best present may be a place beside him in the sleigh.

A new Christmas tradition begins

Christmas is almost here, Santa fretted as he checked the rudders on his sleigh. “What am I to do? I have baby dolls, soccer balls, baseball gloves, bicycles, video games, and golf tees—gifts for everyone except Mrs. Claus. Last year I gave her a diamond necklace from Siberia. The year before that, she liked the African gazelle, even though it was difficult to keep it from leaping out of my sleigh until we reached the North Pole. I always give her rose perfume, but,” he pushed back his red hat to scratch his head, “what can I give her this year? She patiently waits until I return and asks about each child. She wants to know how the trees were decorated, what sort of cookies the children left for me, and if the cocoa was still warm. I appreciate her enthusiasm, but she can’t ride with me, or can she?”

He continued to muse, “I am the boss of Christmas deliveries. If my elves made some small changes to my sleigh, could Mrs. Claus ride with me for just one night?” He continued to stomp around in the snow. “Yes, I’ll have the elves make room for her. She might not agree to go with me, but it’s worth a try since I can’t think of another thing she might like.”

★   ★   ★

On Christmas Eve morning, Santa wished Mrs. Claus, “Merry Christmas,” and pecked her on the cheek as they sat down to breakfast. They feasted on platters of pancakes glistening with maple syrup, and Mrs. Claus gave him red knitted mittens and a warm cozy scarf. He thanked her and led her outside to his sleigh. She gasped when she saw the huge red bow on its front.

“Is the sleigh my present?” she asked, her eyes open wide. “I wouldn’t know what to do with a sleigh, and you’ll need it tonight.”

“No,” he chuckled, “it’s not the sleigh. Here, let me help you climb inside.”

“Oh, is my gift on the cushions?” her hand brushed the soft velvet seat.

“Goodness, no!” he replied. “But my elves did rearrange the sleigh. Sit down and see how it feels.”

Her cheeks flushed with joy as she climbed aboard.

Santa eased himself onto the seat beside her. “See, we fit just fine. Would you like to go with me tonight to see the rooftops, the Eiffel Tower lit up for Christmas, the Empire State Building in…”

“I’ve always dreamed of riding in your sleigh and delivering presents to the good little boys and girls throughout the world.”

Mrs. Claus put her hand on his arm. “Yes! Yes! Of course! I’ve always dreamed of riding in your sleigh and delivering presents to the good little boys and girls throughout the world. But we must hurry. Won’t I need a red suit and hat to wear if I’m going to go down all those chimneys? I wouldn’t want to get soot on my red dress!”

She looked at him, her eyes shining, while Santa’s mouth gaped open as he imagined her delivering gifts, eating his cookies, and drinking his cocoa. He paused in thought. “You know,” he exclaimed, “That might not be a bad idea. I could use a little catnap on that long night’s trip. You might even help me keep an eye out for satellites and weather balloons.” He nodded. “Okay. You have those clothes made, and while the elves are sewing, I’ll give you some pointers.”

“Oh, you don’t need to, My Dear. I read all your notes, and I know just what to do. We’ll have such a good time!” she giggled, clapping her hands in delight. “Let’s get ready!”

★   ★   ★

If you fall asleep on the sofa in front of your fireplace on Christmas Eve, you may be awakened by the sound of soft footfalls and the smell rose perfume. Open your eyes just a bit, and you may see Mrs. Claus taking dainty sips of your cookies and cocoa. Santa has a new partner. Team Santa has come to stay.

Publication Note

This story appeared with illustrations in Smarty Pants Magazine for Kids in December 2021. The magazine was formerly a digital nonfiction publication featuring short stories, book reviews, and contests for young readers.
